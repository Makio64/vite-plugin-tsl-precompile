How this is measured—and why it is not a precompiled benchmark

For each scene we build a TSL material with stock three/webgpu, then time the cold first renderAsync against a trimmed set of 90 warm frames after a 40-frame warm-up. The cold path includes Three's TSL graph walk and WGSL generation, WebGPU pipeline creation, driver work, resource initialization, and other one-time costs.

Caveats: this is wall time around renderAsync, not isolated CPU or GPU time; numbers vary by GPU, driver, browser, and thermal state. A precompiled render still creates and compiles a WebGPU pipeline. Therefore cold minus warm is only a cold-path envelope. A defensible performance claim requires cold stock and cold precompiled runs of the same scene on the same device; this page does not pretend otherwise.

What the architecture guarantees: captured materials use embedded WGSL and generated update plans. With either slim mode (slim: 'source' preferred for new Vite apps), the production bundle uses a NodeBuilder-stripped Three build. Without slim mode, the full compiler may still exist elsewhere in the bundle even though marked materials use their artifacts. See the exact boundary →
